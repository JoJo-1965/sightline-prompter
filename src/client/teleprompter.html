Settings

Scrolling

A sync point for the edit. Before each take the screen flashes white and shows the section and take number for a second; with the beep, your microphone records it too. The take log notes when each slate went off, so every take can be lined up with the recording. Start reading after the beep.

While the prompter runs, Sightline records the microphone to a plain audio file beside the take log, even off script. An editor can line your real recording up with the take log by matching the two soundtracks, with no slate needed. It stays on this computer, and goes into the edit pack when you export one.

Measures your microphone through each take: its loudness, its loudest peak and the room's background noise. A take that clipped, was very quiet or had a noisy room gets a warning as it ends, in the take summary and in the take log and edit pack. It's the mic as Sightline hears it, before OBS or Riverside process it, so treat it as a guide.

Voice scroll sync

The sound-check meter (Sound check in the Voice panel, or Ctrl+Shift+D) shows your mic's level, loudness and the room's background noise at the edge of the prompter. It hides while a take runs unless this is ticked.

Say the wake word (below) then a command, and stop: pause, go, slower, faster, back, next section, from the top, again, marker, resync, bigger, smaller, or what can I say. While steady scrolling, the microphone stays on to listen for them (on this computer only; nothing is sent anywhere).

Choose Voice as the mode on the Scrolling tab to use these.

Your words

Script words

Names and words it still mishears: type your word, then what it hears instead, with commas between. Each counts as your word wherever the script has it, and as itself everywhere else.

Command words

Choose the wake word or a command it misses, then type what it hears, such as “prompt her”.

Teach… listens while you say the word or command a few times, with a pause between, and adds what it hears. Changes apply the next time it starts listening.

Only a device with the code in the QR code can control the prompter. The first time, Windows may ask whether to allow Sightline Prompter on the network: allow it on private networks. New code disconnects any phone already connected. Turn this off when you don't need it.
